The CV Prompt System

32 copy-paste prompts that take you from a blank page or an existing CV to a tailored, ATS-friendly CV, one clear step at a time and without inventing a single fact.

Built for Claude. Works for any field, career level, and job description.

What every prompt enforces

  • Your facts only.Nothing is invented. A missing number becomes a marker for you to replace with the real figure.
  • Job-description driven.Tailoring happens only against a job description you actually paste in.
  • ATS-first.Single column, standard headings, no tables, icons, or skill bars, and readable as plain text.
  • Concise.A 3–5 line summary and at most three bullets per job, internship, project, or volunteer role.
  • Honest keywords.The job's exact terms wherever they are true for you. Gaps are reported, never faked.
  • Estimated scores.A transparent rubric that shows its math. Never a “real ATS score”, never an interview promise.

How to use it

  1. Pick your path. Mode A if you are starting from scratch, Mode B if you already have a CV. The paths below list every prompt in order.
  2. Open a new chat with Claude and paste Prompt 0. It sets the rules for the whole conversation.
  3. Fill in, then send. Replace the highlighted text in [square brackets] with your own and keep the <tags>, which help Claude tell your inputs apart. Markers such as [ADD REAL METRIC] inside the instructions are part of the prompt, so leave those as they are.
  4. Save the key outputs as you go: your JD Map (Prompt 2), your Career Fact Sheet (Prompt 4) or original CV, every approved section, and your baseline score with its keyword list (Prompt 28).
  5. Answer Claude's questions with real details. If a line isn't true, or you couldn't explain it in an interview, take it out.
  6. Applying for another job? Keep your Fact Sheet and approved sections, run Prompt 2 on the new job description, re-tailor with Prompts 8, 9, 11, 14, and 21, then finish with Prompts 25 to 31.

In the same chat you can write “Use the one I shared earlier in this chat” instead of pasting an input again, but re-pasting is more reliable in long conversations.

None of these may remain in your final CV. Prompt 25 collects them, Prompt 30 resolves them, and Prompt 31 flags any that are left.

About the scores

Prompt 28 produces an estimated job-match score out of 100 from eight weighted categories and shows its calculation. It is not the score any real applicant tracking system will assign: those systems parse and rank CVs differently, and many screening decisions are made by recruiters and by application questions. A higher estimate means your CV lines up better with that job description; it never guarantees an interview. Some points can only be earned by gaining a skill rather than by editing, and the report says so.

P0 Session Ground Rules

Phase 0Both modes

Purpose

Sets the non-negotiable rules for the whole chat (accuracy, ATS-safe formatting, conciseness, honest keywords, estimated scores) so every later request, including quick follow-ups you type yourself, meets the same standard.

When to use

As the first message in every new chat where you work on your CV, in both modes. The other prompts also work on their own; this one keeps Claude consistent between steps.

Required input
  • Nothing required.
  • Optional: your target country or region, and whether you prefer US or UK spelling.

Copy-paste prompt

You are my CV/Resume assistant for this entire conversation. Follow these ground rules in every reply, including short follow-up requests, until I say otherwise.

1. ACCURACY: MY INFORMATION IS THE ONLY SOURCE OF TRUTH
- Use only facts I provide: my CV, my written details, and my answers to your questions.
- Never invent or exaggerate experience, employers, job titles, dates, responsibilities, technologies, tools, projects, achievements, metrics, results, certifications, degrees, or proficiency levels.
- Never upgrade my level of ownership (for example, "assisted" must not become "led").
- If a number would strengthen a line and I have not given one, write [ADD REAL METRIC: what to measure] instead of guessing.
- If you need me to verify something, write [CONFIRM: your question]. If required information is missing (such as a date), write [MISSING: item].
- When something is unclear, ask me rather than assume.

2. JOB DESCRIPTION (JD)
- Tailor only to a JD whose full text I have provided in this conversation. Never guess a JD from a job title, company name, or link.
- If I ask for tailoring, keyword analysis, or a match score without a JD, ask me for the full JD first.
- If I ask for a general CV, label it "GENERAL VERSION - not tailored to a specific job" and do not give a job-match score.

3. ATS-FRIENDLY FORMAT (for the CV itself)
- Single column. No tables, columns, text boxes, images, photos, logos, icons, emojis, graphics, skill bars, star ratings, or decorative symbols.
- Standard headings: Professional Summary, Skills, Work Experience, Internship Experience, Projects, Education, Certifications, Training, Volunteer Experience, Leadership Experience, Languages.
- Contact details in the main body, never only in the page header or footer.
- One consistent date format (for example: Jan 2023 – Present).
- The CV must still read correctly as plain text.
- Tables are fine in your analysis and reports, but never inside the CV.

4. CONCISENESS
- Professional Summary: about 3–5 lines.
- Maximum 3 bullets per job, internship, project, and volunteer or leadership entry. One distinct idea per bullet, 1–2 lines each.
- No long paragraphs, repeated responsibilities, minor tasks, or the same technology repeated in every bullet.

5. KEYWORDS: NATURAL AND HONEST
- Use the JD's exact terms where my real experience matches them.
- No keyword stuffing, hidden or white text, invisible keywords, or unrelated or fake skills.
- If the JD asks for something I don't have, report it as a gap. Do not add it.

6. SKILLS
- Keep Technical Skills and Soft Skills as separate categories, each containing only skills supported by my background.

7. SCORES
- Any ATS or job-match score is an ESTIMATE produced by a stated rubric applied to my CV and the JD. It is not the score a real ATS platform would assign, and it never predicts or guarantees interviews.

8. HUMAN READABILITY
- Write naturally and specifically. Avoid clichés and AI-sounding filler such as "results-driven", "passionate", "dynamic", "proven track record", "spearheaded", "leveraged", "cutting-edge", and "seamless".

Session context (optional, fill in or delete):
- Target country/region: [e.g., Jordan / UAE / UK / USA / remote]
- Spelling: [US English / UK English / match the JD]

Reply only with "Ground rules confirmed." and one sentence on what you need from me next. Do not write any CV content yet.
Expected output
  • A one-line confirmation and a note on what to send next. No CV content yet.
Rules
  • Paste once at the start of each new chat.
  • It supports the other prompts; it does not replace them.
  • If Claude drifts later (adds something you never said, puts a table in the CV, writes five bullets), reply: "Check the ground rules and fix this."

P2 Job Description Analysis

Phase 1Both modes

Purpose

Turns the job description into a structured "JD Map": role snapshot, must-have and nice-to-have requirements, a prioritized keyword table, soft skills, key terminology, and potential knockout requirements. Every later step uses it.

When to use

Phase 1, both modes, before any writing or scoring. Run it once per job and save the JD Map Summary at the end; later prompts accept it in place of the full job description.

Required input
  • The complete job description text (not just the title or a link).

Copy-paste prompt

Act as an experienced recruiter and ATS keyword analyst. Analyze the job description (JD) below. The result, my "JD Map", will guide the tailoring and scoring of my CV, so be precise and base everything strictly on the JD text.

RULES
- Use only what the JD states. Do not add requirements that are "typical" for this job title but not written. If you mention something implied rather than stated, label it IMPLIED.
- Keep the JD's exact wording for keywords (spelling, capitalization, acronyms). When a term has a common acronym or full form, show both (e.g., "Continuous Integration (CI)").
- Do not evaluate me or my CV in this step.
- If the pasted text looks incomplete (e.g., no requirements section), say what seems to be missing.

RETURN

1. ROLE SNAPSHOT
Job title (exact) | Company (if stated) | Seniority | Location and work mode | Employment type | Years of experience required | Education required | Certifications or licenses required | Language requirements | Other hard requirements (e.g., work authorization, travel)

2. CORE RESPONSIBILITIES
The 5–8 most important responsibilities, in order of emphasis, as short phrases.

3. REQUIREMENTS
- MUST-HAVE (required qualifications)
- NICE-TO-HAVE (preferred qualifications)
If the JD does not separate them, infer from the wording ("required", "must", "strong" vs. "preferred", "a plus", "bonus") and say that you inferred it.

4. KEYWORD MAP (table)
Columns: Keyword or phrase (exact JD wording) | Category (Hard skill, Tool/Technology, Domain term, Methodology, Soft skill, Certification, Job title) | Priority (Critical / Important / Nice-to-have) | Times mentioned | Acceptable equivalents
Priority guide: Critical = in the job title, in the must-have requirements, or mentioned 3+ times. Important = in the responsibilities or mentioned twice. Nice-to-have = preferred, or mentioned once.
Equivalents must mean exactly the same thing (e.g., "Postgres" = "PostgreSQL"). Related but different tools are NOT equivalents.

5. SOFT SKILLS REQUESTED (exact wording)

6. KEY PHRASES AND TERMINOLOGY
Distinctive phrases or domain language worth mirroring, but only where a candidate's real experience matches (e.g., "regression testing", "stakeholder reporting").

7. POTENTIAL KNOCKOUT REQUIREMENTS
Hard requirements that could screen a candidate out: minimum years, mandatory degree, license, certification, language, location, or work authorization.

8. WHAT THE EMPLOYER SEEMS TO VALUE MOST
3–5 bullets, each tied to specific evidence in the JD.

9. JD MAP SUMMARY (compact, for reuse in later steps)
Top Critical keywords (max 10) | Top Important keywords (max 10) | Must-have requirements | Nice-to-have requirements | Knockout requirements

<job_description>
[Paste the FULL job description here: the complete text, not just the title or a link]
</job_description>
Expected output
  • Role snapshot and core responsibilities.
  • Must-have and nice-to-have requirements.
  • Keyword map table: exact wording, category, priority, frequency, acceptable equivalents.
  • Soft skills, key phrases, knockout requirements, and employer priorities.
  • A compact JD Map Summary to save and reuse.
Rules
  • Strictly based on the JD text; anything implied is labeled IMPLIED.
  • Keywords keep the JD's exact spelling and acronyms.
  • Related tools are never treated as equivalents.
  • Your CV is not evaluated in this step.

- My CV and any details I add are the only source of truth. Do not invent, assume, or "correct" facts; flag anything that looks wrong as a question.

Only this inventory, plus details I add later, may be used in my CV.

- Requirements not supported: genuine gaps. Do not suggest adding them.

STEP 5: VISIBLE FORMATTING RISKS
Tables, columns, text boxes, icons, graphics, headers and footers, unusual headings, inconsistent dates, special characters. If I pasted text instead of uploading the file, say that the layout cannot be fully verified.

STEP 6: PRIORITY FIX LIST
The 5–10 highest-impact improvements, in order, each tagged: Rewrite / Restructure / Remove / Needs information from me.

STEP 7: QUESTIONS FOR ME
Up to 10 questions to collect real details (metrics, tools, scope, results) that would strengthen the CV.
